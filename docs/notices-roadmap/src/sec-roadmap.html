10.1 Sequencing logic

The order below follows the two judges' recommendation (§10.3) and differs from the author's first draft in one respect: the GST team's inbox comes before the redesigned screen. The reasoning: the first three weeks make the numbers right, stop the sync from destroying staff work, close the security holes that no email link should be sent across, and write the module's positions down. Alerting ships next because it is the cheaper half of the ask and it works on the existing lists once the data is trustworthy. The redesigned dashboard, work queue and drawer follow; the matter layer, unattended sync and retention, and MIS complete the module. Each phase is independently shippable and leaves the app better than it found it. Every phase carries a week of slack in its estimate.

10.2 Phase plan

Phase 0 — Stabilise, secure, write it downWeeks 1–3 · ~18 person-days
Goal: a Sync All never destroys staff work or serves as an attack path; every tile has one written definition and reconciles with the lists behind it; the module's positions exist on paper.
0.1 Upsert sync — migration adds portal_key, first_seen_at, last_seen_at, portal_hash, deleted_at and the unique index; replaceNotices, replaceRefundApplications, replaceDrc03Filings and replaceCaseFolderItems become upserts that send portal-owned columns only and soft-mark keys not returned; a run that returns zero rows never marks anything missing. Back-fill portal_key; dedupe case-task rows by ARN.
0.2 Failure path and job lifecycle — a failed pull writes only client_sync_log (action per step: login / notices / refunds / drc03, with a reason class) and never a notice row; delete the five "PULL FAILED" writes; login failures are detected and logged; a top-level catch advances the job instead of stalling it; the 20-minute job expiry becomes idle-based so a 92-client run can finish; the extension announces its version and the app enforces a minimum; every bridge message carries the acting user.
0.3 One canonical notice set — a shared useNoticeSet() hook (fetchAllRows with an explicit ORDER BY id, errors surfaced, sentinel and soft-deleted rows excluded) feeding the tiles, Notice Summary, GSTIN-wise count and the all-clients list. Definitions: Open = not closed; Overdue = open, no reply logged, effective due date (extended or original, parsed as an IST calendar date) before today; Due-in-7 = open, effective due date within 0–7 days; New = first_seen_at within 24 h. A Vitest fixture pins these definitions and the refund / DRC-03 status vocabularies observed on the live portal.
0.4 Closing sweep and due dates — rule-based auto-close for LUT acknowledgements, the 131 DRC-03 acknowledgements and the 22 refunds already closed by an RFD-06 / PMT-03 order that the live check found counted as "open", and case rows whose folder holds a closure item; a bulk "Close with reason" action for the rest. Case/task rows get their due date and hearing date from the folder JSON the extension already stores (159 due dates and 35 hearing dates sit unread today); RFD-08 reply dates are kept instead of dropped.
0.5 Security stop-gaps (hand-verified, §4.2) — add https://gst.vjdesai.com/* to the extension manifest so Sync All works in production; make the app bridge accept messages only from the app's own origin; apply the role from user_roles on session restore instead of trusting the localStorage copy; guard /clients, /add-client and /edit-client for staff roles; make the return-pdfs bucket private, drop the "anyone can upload / delete" storage policies and serve PDFs through short-lived signed URLs; mask portal passwords everywhere and stop copying them into chrome.storage. Credential encryption and Supabase Auth adoption remain a separate project-wide track (§11.4).
0.6 Quick fixes — top-nav and sidebar active states from the route; aria-labels and keyboard access on icon buttons, calendar days and clickable cells; calendar "Both" contrast; real error states; no full-page reload after Import Company; inline status/priority edits that survive re-renders; hooks moved above early returns; the always-empty "Additional Notices" report removed or wired.
0.7 Write it down — docs/NOTICES_LITIGATION_POSITIONS.md recording the category taxonomy, the tile definitions, the refund / DRC-03 dedupe and closure rules, the sync constants and the CAPTCHA posture; a notices section in CLAUDE.md; a one-page staff SOP for Sync All and a runbook for a failed client login; extension/README.md brought up to date.
  • Sync the same client twice: staff_status, priority, assign_to, reply/order fields unchanged; refund documents intact.
  • No row in gst_notices has a description starting "PULL FAILED"; no client is left without history after a failed pull.
  • Total / Open / Overdue on the dashboard equal the row counts of their drill-down lists and of the GSTIN-wise report; the Vitest fixture passes.
  • Sync All starts on gst.vjdesai.com; a client-role login cannot open /clients or read a portal password; a notice PDF URL fetched without a session returns 400/403.
  • npm run build green; the existing routes unchanged.
Phase 1 — Email every important matter to the GST teamWeeks 4–6 · ~16 person-days
Goal: the matrix in §6 is live on the corrected data; nobody on the team needs to open the app to learn that a notice arrived, is due, or was missed.
1.1 Event source — notice_events table and trigger on gst_notices (captured / changed / removed / assigned / status); staff actions write events from the app.
1.2 Rules, queue and outbox — notice_alert_rules, notice_alert_log, staff_notification_prefs; new edge function queue-notice-alerts on a 15-minute cron plus the 09:30 IST digest and Monday MIS runs; email_outbox extended (new kinds, nullable client_id, to/cc arrays, attachments, dedupe_key, a preview status for dry runs); send-gst-email cadence raised to every 15 minutes with attachment support and a "notice" variant of the corporate shell.
1.3 A named owner — assign_to_user_id replaces free-text assign_to (back-filled by name match); a staff picker in the list; "Mine" filter; the ladder escalates to a person, then the team list, then the partner.
1.4 Early statutory clocks — a minimal matter_deadlines table seeded from existing rows without waiting for matters: appeal (s.107) and tribunal (s.112) clocks from order dates and ORDRS folder items, rectification (s.161), RFD-08 reply dates, DRC-22 one-year attachments. E5 limitation alerts fire from this phase; Phase 3 re-parents these rows to matters.
1.5 Templates and a fatigue-safe rollout — E1–E11 seeded in email_templates under a "Notice alerts" tab of the Reminders page; week one runs digest-only with immediate alerts in preview status so the team can read what would have been sent; then per-client batching, an overdue repeat cap, quiet hours and the critical-only exception switch on.
1.6 Client-facing — E12 / E13 templates and a "Request documents from client" action, reusing the client reminder ladder and the per-client on/off switch.
1.7 Visibility — every send appears on the all-clients list (per-row "last alert") and in the outbox log; failures surface on the dashboard. Deep links land on /notices-all?ref=… until the drawer exists in Phase 2.
  • A new DRC-01 captured by sync reaches gst@vjdesai.com within 15 minutes with the PDF attached.
  • Re-running the cron sends nothing twice (dedupe_key); the preview week produces zero unexpected sends.
  • The digest arrives at 09:30 IST every day the data changed; the partner receives the Monday MIS; an order with no appeal filed produces a T-30 limitation email.
Phase 2 — Dashboard redesign, work queue and matter drawerWeeks 7–10 · ~26 person-days
Goal: the page in §5 — a partner opens it and sees what needs action today, who owns it and what money is at stake; every email deep-links into it.
2.1 Shell — new header (sync line, global search, actions), route-driven tabs, URL-persisted filter chips; page root on the app's 8-point rhythm; Company onboarding moved to the Clients page; one react-query hook over a Postgres view / RPC returning aggregates plus the top-N queue instead of four effects pulling the whole table.
2.2 Needs-attention strip — four tiles with the Phase 0 definitions plus ₹ exposure from amount_of_demand; each tile is a saved filter of the queue.
2.3 Work queue and permissions — sortable, filterable table with Team / Mine / Unassigned / Hearings tabs; bulk assign, priority, stage, email-team, export with confirmation and an audit event; permission keys manage_notices, close_matters, partner_review, view_exposure; sensitive writes moved behind SECURITY DEFINER RPCs that validate the app session.
2.4 Charts — ageing buckets, demand-by-stage donut, category bars, top exposure by client (recharts, semantic tokens, click-to-filter).
2.5 Next 14 days and Sync & alerts cards — deadline strip from matter_deadlines, month toggle, sync health with last-sync time and failed logins, activity feed from notice_events.
2.6 Matter drawer v1 — the drawer from §5.4 on a single notice: badges, facts, activity, documents (portal PDF, case-folder attachments, uploads, all via signed URLs), notes, log reply / order / submission with close-with-reason and reopen; replaces the current edit dialog; /notices/:id route for deep links.
2.7 Refunds and DRC-03 as typed, rupee-bearing data — cause of payment, period and SCN linkage kept by the parser; DRC-03 amounts back-filled from the case folders already stored (₹ 6.46 crore across 130 rows shows as zero today); sanctioned_amount populated from RFD-05 / 06; all captured documents reachable; one definition of "closed" for a refund case.
2.8 Responsive and accessible — one-column layout below 1024 px, card list on phones, focus-visible everywhere, contrast checked.
  • Every number on the page opens a list whose count matches it.
  • A notice can be assigned, prioritised, staged and closed with a reason without leaving the dashboard.
  • Lighthouse accessibility ≥ 95; no text below 12 px; first paint under 1.5 s with 5,000 notices.
Phase 3 — Litigation core (3a: matters, deadlines, hearings · 3b: demand, documents, review, client view)Weeks 11–15 · ~36 person-days
Goal: disputes are managed as matters through their statutory lifecycle, with deadlines, hearings, demand and payments in one place, and the client kept informed.
3a.1 Schema and back-fill — tables in §8.1; litigation_rules seeded from §7.1; one-off grouping of existing notices into suggested matters (by case ARN first) with a review page for staff to confirm; Phase 1 deadlines re-parented.
3a.2 Matters page — list and board views by stage; filters by lifecycle, client, owner, officer, FY; matter creation from any notice; stage stepper with computed next step and deadline; stage history; typed manual entry for lifecycles the portal does not expose (appeals, GSTAT, recovery, e-way bill, summons).
3a.3 Deadline engine and hearings — computed vs overriding deadlines with their statutory basis; hearings table, Hearings page with a calendar, ICS export, adjournment logging, documents-to-carry checklist; E4 reminders switched to hearing rows; appeal and rectification clocks created automatically on DRC-07 / APL-04 capture.
3b.1 Demand, payments and outcomes — demand table per Act and head; DRC-03 linkage as payments; pre-deposit calculator from litigation_rules; recovery / stay status; closure rules with outcome (dropped, confirmed, partly, won, lost) so win-rate and time-to-close exist.
3b.2 Documents, reply workspace and review — unified document list with kinds in the private bucket; reply drafting area with firm templates and links to precedent matters; partner review step recorded as an event; authority and counsel masters.
3b.3 Client view — read-only "Notices & litigation" page in the client portal showing stage, next date and documents requested; E12 / E13 wired to it.
  • A DRC-07 captured today automatically shows an appeal last-date and the pre-deposit amount.
  • Every open matter has an owner, a stage and a next date, or appears in an exceptions list.
  • Exposure under dispute on the dashboard equals the sum over open matters; DRC 03 "open" counts only genuinely pending payments.
Phase 4 — Keep it running: unattended sync, retention and recoveryWeeks 16–18 · ~18 person-days
Goal: data is fresh every morning without anyone clicking, failures are visible and specific, and litigation records cannot be lost by a click or a cascade.
4.1 Agent handler — PULL_NOTICES_BUNDLE in agent/src/handlers.ts porting the notices, refunds, DRC-03 and case-folder capture from content.js to Playwright, using the same upsert path; portal_agent migration verified on the live project; portal_jobs writable only through an RPC.
4.2 Scheduling — pg_cron enqueues the bundle for active clients at 06:00 IST; retries with backoff on transient failures; needs_human CAPTCHA queue surfaced on the Sync & alerts card; run summaries with row counts.
4.3 Guardrails — portal-change detection (row shape or zero-row anomalies raise E9), active-flag respected, per-client credential health, session cookies with expiry and rotation.
4.4 Retention and recovery — soft delete on every litigation table; client delete becomes archive-with-audit and offers a non-destructive alternative; orphaned PDFs cleaned up; a documented and tested backup / point-in-time-recovery procedure; a legal-hold flag for matters under dispute (s.36 record-keeping).
  • For 10 consecutive days, 100% of active clients have a sync younger than 24 h or a named failure reason.
  • Zero human CAPTCHA entries on days when sessions are still valid.
  • A deleted client can be restored with every notice, document and log intact.
Phase 5 — MIS, client reporting and polishWeeks 19–20 · ~14 person-days
Goal: the partner and the client each get the report they need without a spreadsheet.
5.1 MIS — exposure by client / stage / officer / section; ageing; opened vs closed; outcomes and time-to-close; per-staff load; all exportable and attached to the Monday email.
5.2 Client litigation status report — per-client PDF on the firm's letterhead (existing report kit) listing open matters, stage, next date, demand and payments; one click from the client profile and from the drawer.
5.3 Polish and options — GSTIN-wise report on the canonical set; in-app notification bell over the same events; optional WhatsApp channel, PDF-to-fields extraction for manual notices, and AI-assisted first-draft replies evaluated as separate decisions.
  • MIS figures match the dashboard; client report generated in one click and matches the drawer.
